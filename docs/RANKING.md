# Ranking decision (M11)

Launch ranked mode is explicitly two-player, registered accounts only, with its own queue. Three/four-player and guest practice remain casual. Winner-only multiplayer placement cannot be treated as several independent equal-strength duels without making arbitrary assumptions about the unfinished losers' order; multiplayer rating is therefore deferred rather than mislabeled Elo.

Use zero-sum Elo starting at 1000, fixed K=32. For winner W and loser L, E(W)=1/(1+10^((L-W)/400)), delta=round(32*(1-E(W))). Add delta to W, subtract the exact same integer from L. Equal ratings transfer16; a400-point upset transfers29. No outcome prediction, purchases or engagement inputs enter the engine/dice. Glicko-2 models uncertainty/inactivity but adds calibration parameters and update periods without current match data; TrueSkill-style multiplayer inference also needs validated finishing positions/priors. Elo is transparent for this constrained launch; it is not a claim of calibrated skill inference in a high-variance dice game.

A server-generated match UUID identifies each final result. In the same transaction as the authoritative final snapshot, INSERT result ON CONFLICT prevents double settlement. Ordered user/rating locks protect simultaneous matches. Only a completed ranked match of two distinct extant accounts updates ratings, ranked games and wins. Casual final results are recorded but give no ranked points. Deleted identities cannot acquire ratings. No client endpoint accepts scores, winners or ratings. Public top100 exposes IDs/names/rating/games/wins, never email, sessions or room seeds; self defaults1000/0/0 until first ranked completion.

Full real-WebSocket ranked and casual games with PostgreSQL test mode separation, every ranked roll proof, repeated/concurrent final writes, zero-sum updates, games/wins and profile privacy. Pure formula tests cover equal ratings/upsets and invalid numeric input. Web/Expo expose ranked quick match and shared profile leaderboard; native device builds remain unverified.

Before public competitive launch: calibrate rating variance, define seasons/divisions and abandonment policy, require verified identities, detect collusion/farming/multiple-account abuse and review audit retention. Current turn timeouts do not award a fabricated victory; an abandoned uncompleted match earns no ranking. Private ranked rooms are currently permitted for development; competitive matchmaking policy needs hardening before release.
